# DRAGON Cover Style Contract

Permanent art direction for DRAGON Daily Newspaper covers. This contract
describes an original identity inspired by the boldness of Moroccan
political-weekly cover design. It does not copy another publication's
masthead, logo, grid, exact composition, or artwork.

## Cover identity

DRAGON should feel like a serious political weekly: investigative,
analytical, visually memorable, and willing to be confrontational or satirical
when the evidence supports it. It is not corporate, generic AI-news design,
or a minimalist newsletter.

Main principle: one strong editorial idea per cover. The reader should grasp
the visual thesis in roughly two or three seconds.

## Color system

Primary colors are black, white, and a strong red accent. Optional backgrounds
include beige, grey, dark blue, green, and muted warm tones. Red is an
emphasis color; it must not cover the whole page by default.

## Layout grammar

For a typical portrait cover:

- Top zone: DRAGON masthead, date, issue number, and two to four compact teasers.
- Side or analysis box: one short analysis teaser in a small bordered box.
- Main hero zone: one dominant image, portrait, illustration, or visual metaphor receiving roughly 55-70% of visual attention.
- Main headline: short and forceful, usually two to six words, in very large type; one key phrase may be red.
- Standfirst: a short three-to-six-line Darija Latin summary explaining the angle without becoming an article.
- Bottom rail: two to four secondary stories, with a Tarikh or Adab teaser when appropriate; use separators and an optional QR/CTA area sparingly.

Do not make a collage of unrelated stories merely because the edition has many
sections.

## Typography

Typography should be bold, high-contrast, editorial, poster-like, and readable
on a phone, PDF page, and thumbnail. Use strong or compressed type where it
helps hierarchy, but never use decorative fonts that damage readability. Do
not reproduce the exact typography of a reference publication.

## Cover modes

### 1. Portrait dossier

Use when one person or institution dominates the lead story. A large portrait,
controlled background, big headline, and restrained margin teasers are typical.

### 2. Symbolic editorial

Use when an idea is stronger than a literal event photograph. Possible
metaphors include an object, flower, mask, mirror, key, card, rope, broken
symbol, animal symbolism, split object or face, or architecture. The metaphor
must be understandable and directly tied to the article thesis.

### 3. Satirical caricature

Use for political absurdity, contradiction, hypocrisy, or institutional
failure when supported by the journalism. Satire must remain identifiable as
editorial illustration and must not create unsupported defamatory claims.

### 4. Dramatic current-event

Use when a real current event has a strong visual or emotional core. Prefer an
expressive editorial composition. Never generate fake documentary evidence;
generated art must remain clearly editorial rather than pretending to be an
authentic news photograph.

## Headline rules

The main headline should normally express a verified editorial angle, not a
bureaucratic announcement such as “Bank Al-Maghrib announces...”. Structures
such as these are examples only:

- “ra9m ma kay7kich l9issa kamla”
- “wa3d kbir... natija sghira”
- “fin salat lflous?”
- “lma kayn9s... walakin 3lach?”

The cover must not invent an accusation or certainty unsupported by the final
edition.

## Language

All ordinary visible cover text is Moroccan Darija written in Latin
characters. Arabic script is prohibited. English or French may remain only for
unavoidable proper nouns, official names, or technical terms. Do not use
technical backend vocabulary such as APIs, pipelines, runners, commits, or
validators on the cover.

## Visual-selection checklist

Before generating a cover, the Cover Director records answers to:

1. What is the single most important story?
2. What is the strongest defensible editorial angle?
3. What emotion should the cover communicate?
4. Is portrait, symbol, satire, or dramatic current-event the best mode?
5. What visual metaphor expresses the article without inventing facts?
6. Which three to five secondary stories deserve teaser space?

The cover director reads the final edition and its sources first and does not
research unrelated new stories.

## Prohibited behavior

Do not copy another newspaper's logo or exact design; use Arabic script; make a
generic AI collage; overload the page; write long article text; use weak
corporate infographic aesthetics; fabricate crimes or scandals; present
generated art as documentary evidence; use gratuitous violence; or sacrifice
readability for visual effects.

## Output

Preferred format is a portrait close to 3:4, approximately 1200 x 1600 pixels
or an equivalent high-resolution ratio. The cover must remain legible on a
phone screen, in the fixed-layout PDF, and in archive thumbnails. Generated
visuals are labelled as editorial illustration.
